# Content map — Physiotherapy English

The first version deliberately covers the themes visible in the Moodle screenshots and extends them into physiotherapy-specific clinical English.

## Vocabulary areas

1. **Body & regions** — trunk, upper/lower limb, thorax, pelvis, distal.
2. **Skeleton & bones** — common + scientific terminology on the same card where useful: skull/cranium, collarbone/clavicle, shoulder blade/scapula, breastbone/sternum, thigh bone/femur, kneecap/patella.
3. **Joints & movement** — flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, dorsiflexion.
4. **Muscles & soft tissue** — tendon, ligament, quadriceps, hamstrings, calf muscles, rotator cuff.
5. **Respiratory tract** — airway, trachea/windpipe, bronchi, diaphragm, inhalation, exhalation.
6. **Blood vessels & circulation** — artery, vein, capillary, pulse, blood pressure, circulation.
7. **Digestive & abdominal** — oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine/colon, liver, abdominal quadrants.
8. **Symptoms & injury** — sprain, strain, swelling/oedema, stiffness, numbness, tingling.
9. **Assessment & function** — range of motion, gait, balance, posture, muscle strength, mobility.
10. **Rehabilitation & equipment** — crutches, walking frame/walker, resistance band, weight-bearing, exercise prescription, home exercise programme.

Every vocabulary entry includes the five requested elements: **audio pronunciation, English term, English definition, French translation and illustration**, plus a clinical example and CEFR indication.

## Grammar pathway

Every grammar module systematically contains:

- a concise lesson;
- physiotherapy examples;
- a BBC Learning English video;
- a 4-question physiotherapy recap quiz.

Modules in v1.0:

1. Question forms for assessment.
2. Present Simple vs Present Continuous.
3. Past Simple vs Present Perfect.
4. Modals & imperatives for instructions.
5. Comparatives for progress & function.
6. Passive voice for procedures.
7. Zero & First Conditionals for safety.

## Diagnostic design

The diagnostic has **34 questions**: two questions from every one of the ten vocabulary areas and two questions from every one of the seven grammar modules. The result is broken down by area and automatically points students to the weakest sections to revise.
